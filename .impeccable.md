## Design Context

### Users

Harriett serves three roles inside a paper-first real estate brokerage:

- Agents work primarily from their phones between appointments. They need quick answers,
  clear next actions, and confidence that longer work has been saved for later review.
- Coordinators work primarily from a desktop. They repeatedly scan many deals, documents,
  deadlines, and handoffs, and need to identify exceptions without opening every record.
- Brokers work primarily from a desktop or tablet. They need approvals, compliance risks,
  pipeline health, and a trustworthy history of what Harriett did and why.

The shared job is to move pre-listing and transaction work forward without asking people to
adopt an entirely new way of communicating. Chat is the conversational entry point. The
dashboard is the durable place to inspect, edit, approve, and complete the work.

### Brand Personality

Calm, capable, and familiar. Harriett is from Tuscaloosa, just like Pritchett-Moore. She
should feel like an experienced local coordinator who knows the office, the market, and how
people there actually work. The voice is professional but folksy, direct without being cold,
and confident without pretending uncertain data is authoritative.

### Aesthetic Direction

Harriett already has a recognizable look: an illustrated woman with a dark high bun, golden
glasses, a crimson houndstooth headband, a black serif wordmark, and a crimson period. That
identity is the visual anchor. The product should feel Tuscaloosa-born and PM-adjacent during
the pilot, not like a generic white-label SaaS dashboard or an AI chat toy.

Use a light theme suited to offices and daylight phone use, warm neutral surfaces, black ink,
restrained crimson accents, compact typography, and clear tabular rhythm. The portrait should
appear selectively as Harriett's identity, especially in conversation, onboarding, and key
empty states. It should not become a repeated decorative badge on every screen.

The interface should remain recognizably Harriett as the product expands to other
brokerages. Tenant theming may add brokerage marks and a small supporting palette, but it
must not erase Harriett's face, name, voice, or core visual character. Avoid oversized
marketing composition, decorative card grids, excessive rounded containers, gradients,
glass effects, and chatbot-first layouts.

### Design Principles

1. Show what needs attention before showing what merely exists.
2. Keep chat brief and make every substantial result a durable, linked work object.
3. Reveal source, freshness, confidence, and approval state wherever they affect trust.
4. Give each role a focused home while preserving one shared operating model.
5. Optimize agent views for quick phone use and coordinator views for dense desktop scanning.
6. Keep Harriett's Tuscaloosa identity intact as brokerage branding is layered around her.
7. Keep external systems visible and connected without copying their entire interfaces.
8. Use plain language from the brokerage's real workflow, not generic CRM terminology.
