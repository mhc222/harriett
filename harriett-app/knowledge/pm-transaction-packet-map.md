# Pritchett-Moore Transaction Packet Knowledge Map

Prepared 2026-08-25.

## Purpose and provenance

This is a privacy-safe operating map derived from sample Pritchett-Moore listing and sales packets, then checked against the cited official sources. It records reusable document types, workflow signals, extraction fields, and review logic. It does not retain the names, addresses, prices, dates, signatures, account numbers, or other facts from the completed sample transaction.

The sample packets are evidence of how one or more files were assembled. They are not proof that every page is current, legally required in every transaction, or part of permanent office policy. Wilson Moore or another authorized broker must approve office-policy conclusions.

## Source hierarchy

When sources disagree, use this order:

1. The complete, signed, current transaction document for facts and obligations in that transaction.
2. Current federal law, Alabama law, and Alabama Real Estate Commission rules for legal requirements.
3. Current written broker-approved Pritchett-Moore policy for office procedure.
4. Current lender, title, closing, MLS, or vendor evidence for third-party facts.
5. Sample or legacy office packets for workflow discovery only.
6. Conversation history and personal memory for continuity and preferences, never as proof.

Harriett must never silently resolve a conflict. She must identify the conflicting sources and request human review.

## Transaction lifecycle

### Relationship

- Identify the consumer and whether the work is covered by RECAD.
- Identify the role being offered: transaction facilitator, single agent, subagent, limited consensual dual agent, or another permitted relationship.
- Deliver the current Alabama Real Estate Brokerage Services Disclosure and the broker-approved office policy when required.
- Record acknowledgement or a refusal notation. Do not treat a missing consumer signature alone as proof the disclosure was not made.

### Pre-listing

- Confirm seller identity, ownership, authority to sell, property identity, and representation.
- Complete the listing agreement and any applicable listing addenda.
- Collect property information, lead disclosure material when applicable, compensation terms, included or excluded items, showing instructions, and marketing permissions.
- Check every page, signature, date, election, handwritten change, initial, and incorporated addendum.

### Listing active

- Confirm that signed listing terms match MLS fields, public remarks, price, dates, property facts, and status.
- Preserve an MLS snapshot as supporting evidence, but never let MLS override a signed agreement.
- Track photos, marketing, seller communications, and internal checklist evidence under current office policy.

### Offer

- Confirm buyer representation and written brokerage agreement timing when applicable.
- Capture every material purchase-agreement term, selected option, deadline, signature, date, and addendum.
- Prepare or retain the required Estimated Closing Statement for each written offer or counteroffer in a single-family residential sale.
- Determine whether lead, FHA, agency, or other conditional forms apply from transaction facts. Never infer applicability from a form merely appearing in a template packet.

### Under contract

- Treat the fully signed agreement and acceptance evidence as the controlling source for contractual obligations.
- Reconcile earnest money, financing, inspections, appraisal, closing, possession, credits, included items, title, agency, and addenda across all documents.
- Generate tasks only from verified dates or clearly labeled broker-approved office reminders.
- Reevaluate conditional forms when financing, price, agency, property facts, or parties change.

### Pre-closing

- Verify final lender and settlement documents against the signed agreement and amendments.
- Confirm the current Closing Disclosure when TRID applies and the final settlement statement or ALTA document used by the closing workflow.
- Flag unresolved differences in price, credits, commissions, payoffs, cash to close, seller proceeds, closing date, or party names.

### Closed

- Retain the final signed contract package and final accounting documents.
- Confirm the transaction status and closing facts before updating MLS or marking tasks complete.
- Preserve audit evidence showing who completed, approved, or waived each workflow item.

## Document families and applicability

### Alabama Real Estate Brokerage Services Disclosure, commonly called RECAD

Applies to covered brokerage services for an individual consumer, subject to statutory exceptions and different property-management treatment. It should be provided before brokerage services are supplied and before confidential information is disclosed. The current rule does not make the consumer's signature a legal prerequisite, but a refusal should be documented.

Extract: licensee, consumer, services or roles disclosed, licensee signature and date, consumer acknowledgement, refusal notation, form version, and delivery evidence.

Official sources:

- Alabama Code Section 34-27-82: https://arec.alabama.gov/pages/laws/ViewLaw.aspx?AspxAutoDetectCookieSupport=1&LawSectionID=28
- Alabama Administrative Code Rule 790-X-3-.13: https://arec.alabama.gov/pages/laws/ViewLaw.aspx?AspxAutoDetectCookieSupport=1&LawSectionID=181
- Current RECAD form: https://arec.alabama.gov/docs/RECADForm09192025.pdf

### Agency and Brokerage Office Policy

Use the current broker-approved office policy with the RECAD workflow when required. Extract the brokerage services offered, compensation explanation, acknowledgement, refusal notation, version, and date. The sample packet cannot establish that an old office-policy page remains current.

Official source:

- Alabama Administrative Code Rule 790-X-3-.14: https://arec.alabama.gov/docs/agency-brokerage-office-policy-emergency-rule09232025.pdf

### Listing agreement

Expected for a represented seller listing. Extract sellers, ownership and authority statements, brokerage, agent, property, legal description when present, term, effective date, expiration, list price, compensation, included and excluded items, marketing permissions, agency terms, dispute terms, signatures, dates, initials, changes, and incorporated addenda.

Execution checks: all pages present, all owners or authorized signers accounted for, term complete, compensation complete, changes initialed and dated, seller signatures present, brokerage acceptance present, and addenda consistent.

### Buyer agency agreement

Expected before submitting an offer for a represented individual buyer when the Alabama brokerage-agreement rule applies. Extract buyers, brokerage, designated agent, term, territory or property scope, services, compensation, exclusivity, termination, signatures, and dates.

Do not infer buyer representation from an agent's name on a purchase agreement.

### Purchase agreement

Expected for a written offer or an under-contract transaction. Extract:

- Buyer and seller legal names
- Property address and legal description or parcel reference
- Purchase price and payment structure
- Earnest money amount, holder, delivery terms, and disposition
- Financing type, loan amount or percentage, down payment, cash terms, and lender conditions
- Closing date, possession, prorations, and closing agent or attorney when stated
- Inspection election, inspection period, repair terms, and termination rights
- Appraisal, financing, title, survey, home warranty, and sale-of-other-property contingencies
- Seller credits, concessions, allowances, and cost allocation
- Included and excluded fixtures or personal property
- Agency disclosure and compensation terms
- Additional provisions and incorporated addenda
- Offer date, expiration, counteroffer history, acceptance date, signatures, initials, and changes

The contract must be reviewed page by page. Boilerplate describes possible terms, but checked boxes, filled blanks, addenda, initials, and signatures determine what was selected.

### Estimated Closing Statement, often called a net sheet

For a single-family residential sale, Alabama Rule 790-X-3-.04 requires the licensee to furnish a complete Estimated Closing Statement each time the licensee prepares or presents a written offer or counteroffer. It should use the best estimates available, be acknowledged by dated signature, and be retained. A lender estimate does not replace it.

Extract: party, property, offer amount, estimated debit and credit items, estimated amounts, preparer, preparation date, and dated acknowledgement.

Official source:

- Alabama Administrative Code Rule 790-X-3-.04: https://arec.alabama.gov/pages/laws/ViewLaw.aspx?AspxAutoDetectCookieSupport=1&LawSectionID=93

### Lead-Based Paint Disclosure and pamphlet

Generally applies to most residential housing built before 1978, subject to federal exemptions. Extract the property, seller knowledge selection, record or report selection, reports supplied, buyer acknowledgement, agent acknowledgement, warning statement, inspection opportunity or written modification, signatures, and dates. Record delivery of the approved pamphlet.

The presence of pamphlet pages inside an office packet does not prove they were delivered to the buyer.

Official source:

- EPA Lead-Based Paint Disclosure Rule: https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-based-paint-disclosure-rule-section-1018-title-x

### FHA Amendatory Clause and Real Estate Certification

Conditionally applicable when FHA financing is used and no HUD exception applies. Extract borrowers, sellers, property, sales price, contract date, amendatory language, certification, signatures, and dates. Recheck the form after a material price or financing change.

Harriett must not decide automatically that a changed transaction requires re-execution. She should request immediate lender and broker review.

Official source:

- HUD FHA sales contract guidance: https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/4155-2_6_SECA.PDF

### Dual Agency Agreement

Conditionally applicable when the permitted relationship is limited consensual dual agency. Extract buyers, sellers, property, agent, brokerage, informed consent, signatures, and dates. Do not infer dual agency merely because both agents work for the same brokerage.

### Designated Single Agency Agreement

Conditionally applicable when different licensees under the same qualifying broker are designated to represent opposing parties. Extract the qualifying broker, company, designated seller agent, designated buyer agent, clients, property, signatures, and dates. Broker confirmation controls the exact relationship.

### Seller Property Information Sheet

Useful office and transaction support material, but the sample does not establish that it is universally required by Alabama law. Extract property features, utilities, systems, known conditions, included and excluded items, explanations, unanswered fields, signatures, and date.

### Office Exclusive Listing Addendum

Apply only when the current listing arrangement or broker-approved office policy requires it. Extract referenced listing agreement, changed terms, seller, property, signatures, dates, and consistency with the main listing agreement.

### Closing Disclosure

Produced by the lender or closing workflow for most covered consumer mortgage loans. Generally, the consumer must receive it at least three business days before consummation. Exceptions and special loan types exist.

Extract: borrowers, sellers, property, loan terms, sale price, closing and disbursement dates, closing costs, credits, cash to close, seller proceeds, and settlement agent. Confirm the latest version and review material changes.

Official source:

- CFPB Closing Disclosure guidance: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-closing-disclosure-en-1983/

### Settlement statement or ALTA statement

Third-party final accounting for the closing. Extract parties, property, closing date, sale price, debits, credits, commissions, payoffs, cash to or from parties, and settlement agent. Confirm the final version and reconcile it to the contract and amendments.

Names such as HUD, ALTA, settlement statement, and Closing Disclosure are not interchangeable in every transaction.

### MLS property record

Supporting property and status evidence. Extract MLS number, capture date, status at capture, address, list price, listing date, agent, brokerage, remarks, and material features. The signed agreement controls if MLS conflicts with transaction documents.

### Internal listing, pending, and closed checklists

The samples show a useful three-stage coordinator workflow. Convert each item into a digital task with stage, owner role, trigger, due rule, evidence, completion time, and exception reason.

Legacy references to paper folders, Excel reports, Instanet, named employees, postcards, and handwritten checkmarks are observations. They require broker confirmation before becoming permanent product rules.

## Packet completeness algorithm

1. Identify the transaction and known facts: side, representation, property type, property year, financing, agency relationship, offer status, lifecycle stage, and closing status.
2. Split the uploaded packet into document boundaries. Record filename, one-based PDF page range, likely document type, form title, form version, and extraction confidence.
3. Determine applicability independently for each document rule.
4. Classify each rule as `applies`, `not_applicable`, or `needs_facts`.
5. For an applicable rule, classify the document as `present_complete`, `present_incomplete`, `present_unreadable`, or `missing`.
6. Verify execution separately from presence. Check pages, signatures, dates, initials, selections, blanks, exhibits, addenda, amendments, and delivery evidence.
7. Reconcile material facts across documents.
8. Report blockers, flags, informational gaps, and the specific fact or page needed to resolve each uncertainty.

Never report a document as missing solely because OCR did not find it. Weak OCR means `present_unreadable` or `needs human review` until the page images are inspected.

## Cross-document reconciliation

Compare at minimum:

- Party names and signing authority
- Property address, parcel, and legal description
- List price, purchase price, and amended price
- Earnest money amount, holder, and delivery term
- Financing type, loan amount, and financing changes
- Seller credits, concessions, allowances, and closing-cost allocation
- Effective date, acceptance date, contingency dates, closing date, and possession date
- Included and excluded items
- Agency relationships, designated agents, and brokerages
- Compensation terms when Harriett has permission to access them
- Addenda and amendments referenced by the contract
- Closing Disclosure and settlement figures against the final signed agreement

A mismatch is not permission to choose one value. Identify both values with their sources and request review.

## Extraction confidence and evidence

Every extracted value should retain:

- Normalized value
- Exact source text when available
- Document ID and filename
- One-based PDF page number
- Bounding box or text offset when the parser supplies it
- Extraction method: embedded text, OCR, vision review, or human entry
- Confidence: high, medium, low, or unreadable
- Review status and reviewer

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. Do not convert low-confidence OCR into a legal conclusion.

## Anti-hallucination rules

- Do not invent a deadline when the signed contract is unreadable or silent.
- Do not treat a typical range as a contractual obligation.
- Do not infer a checked option from nearby boilerplate.
- Do not infer execution from a blank template or unsigned copy.
- Do not infer delivery from a form being stored in the packet.
- Do not infer representation, dual agency, or designated agency from agent names alone.
- Do not infer that a historical office form is current.
- Do not use memory, chat history, or an MLS sheet to override a signed agreement.
- Do not call legal advice or make a legal conclusion. Surface evidence, uncertainty, and the broker-review path.

## Privacy and reuse

- Do not use completed client documents as templates.
- Do not ingest sample-client PII into shared knowledge, personal memory, or model training.
- Store the generalized document map separately from transaction records.
- Keep actual contract pages private, office-scoped, and transaction-scoped.
- Retrieve only the pages needed for the current question and audit the retrieval.
- Preserve source versions so a later form or rule change does not rewrite historical reasoning.

## Product behavior derived from the samples

Harriett should be able to:

- Identify documents and page ranges inside a mixed transaction packet.
- Map contract data into people, property, deal, money, dates, conditions, agency, document, and task records.
- Explain what is present, incomplete, unreadable, missing, not applicable, or undecidable from current facts.
- Cite the exact document and page for every transaction claim.
- Generate a digital listing, pending, or closed checklist from verified transaction facts.
- Recheck applicable forms when financing, agency, price, parties, or dates change.
- Detect cross-document mismatches without silently correcting them.
- Route legal, compliance, and ambiguous execution questions to broker review.

This map is reusable knowledge. The original sample transaction remains private evidence and is not part of the shared knowledge corpus.
