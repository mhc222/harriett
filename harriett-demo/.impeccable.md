## Design Context

### Users
Real estate agents and brokers at Pritchett-Moore Real Estate in Tuscaloosa, Alabama. Paper-first office, range from AI-eager to AI-resistant. Use on desktop during the workday to coordinate active transactions and prepare for listing appointments. Goal: save time, catch things they'd miss, not look like software.

### Brand Personality
Professional, folksy, trustworthy. Alabama-grounded. Not chirpy, not robotic. Harriett feels like a sharp assistant who grew up in the same town.

Three words: **warm, precise, southern**

### Aesthetic Direction
Light mode — used in an office during daylight hours. Warm cream and ink palette already established. Crimson accent is restrained and purposeful. Geist Sans body, Playfair Display headings. The UI should feel like a well-designed legal pad or a quality real estate brokerage letterhead — not a SaaS dashboard, not a startup. Intentional whitespace. Understated over flashy.

Anti-references: No cyan/purple AI gradients. No glowing dark-mode dashboards. No hero metric tiles with big numbers and sparklines.

### Design Principles
1. **Understated authority** — The interface should feel like it belongs in a brokerage office, not a tech startup. Restraint builds trust.
2. **Warm empty states** — When there's nothing to show, the UI should feel welcoming and instructive, not broken.
3. **No redundancy** — Every element earns its place. If it's in the nav, it's not also a button on the page.
4. **Harriett has a voice** — Empty states speak in first person, warmly, like Harriett would say it in a text message.
5. **Space is intentional** — Don't fill space with noise. A clean empty state is better than a cluttered one.
